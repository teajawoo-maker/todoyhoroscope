How to Find Your Chinese Zodiac Sign by Birth Date

Find your Chinese zodiac sign without missing the Lunar New Year boundary, especially for January and February birthdays.

Your Chinese zodiac sign is commonly associated with a lunar-calendar year, not simply the Gregorian year printed on a birth certificate. That distinction matters most for people born in January or February, when Lunar New Year may not have started yet.

A reliable three-step check

  1. Write your complete birth date, including the year.
  2. Check the Lunar New Year date for that year using a reliable calendar or cultural reference.
  3. If your birthday came before that boundary, check the preceding lunar year rather than assuming the January 1 year applies.

This avoids the most common mistake in quick zodiac-year charts. Traditions can also differ in detail, so treat a sign as cultural context and a reflection prompt, not a scientific personality diagnosis.

Why the Lunar New Year boundary changes the answer

The sequence of Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig repeats across lunar years. Because the new lunar year begins on a changing date, two people born in the same Gregorian year can fall on different sides of that boundary.

Two examples that show why the month matters

A person born in July can usually use the ordinary Gregorian-year cycle as a quick reference because Lunar New Year has already passed. A person born on February 1 cannot safely do that without checking the year's boundary date. The sign shown by a year-only calculator may still belong to the preceding lunar year.

For a boundary birthday, write down the full date, open the Hong Kong Observatory's Gregorian-Lunar conversion resources, and compare the birthday with Lunar New Year's Day for that year. Keep the source and date with your result if you are using it for family history or a cultural project.

Frequently asked questions

Does a zodiac sign scientifically determine personality?

No. This site presents zodiac signs as cultural context and reflection prompts, not as validated personality tests.

Why do different websites sometimes show different signs?

Many quick charts assign a sign from January 1, while lunar-year references use Lunar New Year. Regional traditions and translations can also differ. Check the calendar boundary and identify the tradition a source is describing.
