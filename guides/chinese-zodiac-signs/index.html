Chinese Zodiac Signs: A Practical Guide to All 12 Animals

Understand all 12 Chinese zodiac signs, the Lunar New Year boundary, and how to use a daily horoscope as a practical reflection prompt.

Published October 15, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

The Chinese zodiac is a repeating 12-sign tradition associated with lunar calendar years. This guide gives English-speaking readers a clear map of the signs and explains how this site turns each profile into a practical reflection prompt. It does not claim that a sign determines personality or guarantees an outcome.

The 12 Chinese zodiac signs in order

The order is Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. The Smithsonian's Lunar New Year resource documents the same sequence and provides cultural collection material for further reading.

How to check your zodiac year

A quick birth-year chart can be misleading for birthdays near Lunar New Year, because the zodiac year does not begin on January 1. If your birthday falls near that boundary, confirm the lunar-year date with a reliable calendar before choosing a sign. Different Asian traditions may also use different zodiac details.

How Today Horoscope writes a daily reading

Each daily sign page is rebuilt by a script once a day, after midnight New York time. Its only inputs are the sign and the date. For each sign it picks from a fixed set of written passages (six strengths, six cautions, and six options each for work, money, love, wellbeing, and timing) and adds a shared daily theme, dealt out so that no two signs get the same theme on the same day. It does not calculate planetary positions, birth charts, or BaZi pillars, and the same date always produces the same text.

Before each update an automated check confirms that all 12 pages are complete, that they keep their entertainment and professional-advice notes, and that no two signs share a reading sentence that day, apart from the fixed safety notes. It cannot check whether a reading will come true, and the site does not claim that one will. The About page explains who runs the site and how to report an error.

How to read each section of a daily page

The daily theme and snapshot suggest one focus. Test it against something already on your calendar instead of looking for signs that it came true. Work, money, love, and wellbeing are prompts for ordinary decisions, not forecasts, so skip any line that does not fit your situation. Best timing is about pacing the day, not lucky hours. The checklist and reflection prompt work best answered in a sentence or two in the evening; the five-minute reflection method shows one way to do it.

Use the reading as a question, not a verdict

Start with a real decision already in front of you. Separate facts from assumptions, choose one reversible next step, and ignore any prompt that does not fit your situation. For medical, legal, financial, or mental-health decisions, use a qualified professional.
