Rabbit compatibility in the classical system

San He places the Rabbit in the Wood trine (亥卯未) with the Pig and the Goat. Its Liu He match is the Dog (卯戌). Across the wheel sits the Rooster, its clash pair (卯酉), which is the classic east-west opposition of the branch compass.

Rabbit in folklore and culture

Chinese folklore puts a rabbit on the Moon. Chang'e, who stole the elixir of immortality from her husband and fled there, lives with a toad and a rabbit that grinds the elixir's ingredients with a pestle and mortar. The Metropolitan Museum of Art holds a Tang-dynasty bronze mirror from the 8th or 9th century that shows the scene.[2] A similar 8th-century mirror at the Art Institute of Chicago is catalogued with a hare instead, so English sources use both words for this animal.[3]

That rabbit, Yutu or Jade Rabbit, gave its name to China's first lunar rover, part of the Chang'e-3 mission, in November 2013. China's space agency says the name was selected following an online poll; its announcement lists 649,956 of 3,445,248 valid votes for Yutu, first among ten finalists, followed by a final round that weighted jury and public votes.[4][5]

In the British Library's version of the race, the Rabbit finishes fourth after washing ashore on a drifting log, looking very surprised. The Dragon, who came fifth, explains that it stopped to blow that log to the bank.[6]

Vietnam's version of the cycle uses a cat instead of a rabbit, as the Victoria and Albert Museum points out.[7]

Under the Vietnamese calendar the same years and start dates belong to the Cat.
