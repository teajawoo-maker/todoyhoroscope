Monkey compatibility in the classical system

The Monkey belongs to the Water trine (申子辰) with the Rat and the Dragon. Its Liu He partner is the Snake (巳申). The Tiger sits across the wheel and forms its six-clash (寅申).

Monkey in folklore and culture

In the Hong Kong Education Bureau's school telling of the race, the Monkey arrives later with the Goat and the Rooster. The three build a raft together, and the Emperor, pleased that they helped one another, places the Monkey ninth, between the Goat and the Rooster.[2]

The Monkey King comes from the novel Journey to the West. Taipei's National Palace Museum describes the edition printed by the Shidetang Hall of Jinling in 1592 as the earliest version of the 100-chapter novel; only four copies survive, three in Japan and one in the museum.[3] The story travelled well beyond China: The Metropolitan Museum of Art holds a Japanese surimono print by Yashima Gakutei, probably from 1824, titled The Monkey King Songokū, from the Chinese novel Journey to the West.[4]

The Met also holds a Kangxi-period porcelain monkey, about 15 cm tall and painted in enamels on the biscuit, made at Jingdezhen in the late 17th or early 18th century.[5] In the United States, the Postal Service marked the Monkey year that ran from February 8, 2016 to January 27, 2017 with the ninth stamp of its Celebrating Lunar New Year series, which reused Clarence Lee's cut-paper monkey and the character for monkey in grass-style calligraphy by Lau Bun.[6]
