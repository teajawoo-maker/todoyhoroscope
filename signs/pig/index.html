Pig compatibility in the classical system

The Pig sits in the Wood trine (亥卯未) with the Rabbit and the Goat. Its Liu He is with the Tiger (寅亥). The Snake is directly opposite, forming its six-clash (巳亥).

Pig in folklore and culture

The Pig is the last to arrive in the Hong Kong Education Bureau's telling of the race. It explains that it was so hungry it stopped to eat and then fell asleep, and it still becomes the twelfth animal of the cycle.[2]

The Victoria and Albert Museum notes that the animals changed as the calendar spread through East and Central Asia: the Turkic peoples of Central Asia replaced the pig with an elephant.[3]

English names vary. The U.S. Postal Service called its 2019 stamp the Year of the Boar, the 12th and final stamp of its Celebrating Lunar New Year series, for the year from February 5, 2019 to January 24, 2020; Kam Mak's art shows pink peach blossoms, which often bloom just in time for the new year and mark the start of spring.[4] The Metropolitan Museum of Art, by contrast, catalogues the matching Kangxi-period porcelain figure, 15.2 cm tall, as a pig.[5]
