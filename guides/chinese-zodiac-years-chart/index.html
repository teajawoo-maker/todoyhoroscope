Before using the chart

If you were born in January or February, check the exact Lunar New Year date for your birth year. A person born before that boundary usually belongs to the preceding zodiac year. The Hong Kong Observatory publishes Gregorian-Lunar conversion tables that can help verify a boundary date.

Check the Hong Kong Observatory calendar resources, then use our birth-date guide.

How the repeating cycle is calculated

The animal sequence repeats every 12 years. In the chart above, 2020 is used as a Rat-year reference, and the sequence moves forward one sign for each Gregorian year. Subtracting or adding 12 returns to the same animal label. That arithmetic is useful for a quick chart, but it does not move the lunar boundary to January 1.

Example: two birthdays in 2008

2008 is a Rat year, but the Rat year began on February 7, 2008. Someone born January 30, 2008 is a Pig (the Pig year ran Feb 18, 2007 to Feb 6, 2008); someone born February 7, 2008 is a Rat. The table below gives the same start and end dates for every year from 1924 to 2031.

Exact start and end dates, 1924–2031

The chart above is the shortcut. This table is the answer. Each row is one lunar year: the stem-branch name and the start date come straight from the Hong Kong Observatory table for that year, and the end date is the day before the next year's Lunar New Year. The element column is the element of the year's heavenly stem (Jia and Yi are Wood, Bing and Ding Fire, Wu and Ji Earth, Geng and Xin Metal, Ren and Gui Water), as listed in the Qing court calendar manual Yuding Xingli Kaoyuan (1713), chapter 1. That is where labels like "Fire Horse" for 2026 come from.
