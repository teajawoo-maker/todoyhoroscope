Goat (Sheep) Chinese Zodiac: Goat Years 1931–2027, Exact Start Dates, Earth Element & Matches

Goat or Sheep years 1931–2027 with exact Lunar New Year start dates, the sign's yin Earth element, Rabbit and Pig trine, Horse pairing and Ox clash.

Published October 15, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026

The eighth sign is written 羊 yáng and goes by Goat, Sheep or Ram in English. Its branch is 未 (wèi), the 1 p.m. to 3 p.m. double hour. The fixed element is Earth, yin. The Chinese word covers the whole goat-and-sheep subfamily; the more specific terms are 山羊 for goats and 綿羊 for sheep.

Goat compatibility in the classical system

San He puts the Goat in the Wood trine (亥卯未) with the Rabbit and the Pig. Its Liu He is with the Horse (午未). Its opposite, and six-clash, is the Ox (丑未).

Goat in folklore and culture

The Han-dynasty dictionary Shuowen Jiezi defines 羊 with a single word, 祥, 'auspicious'. Taiwan's Ministry of Education dictionary adds that 羊 could stand in for 祥 in old writing, quoting an inscription recorded in a Song-dynasty collection that ends 大吉羊, 'great good fortune'.[2]

The same dictionary cites the Han thinker Wang Chong's Lunheng for the branch pairing itself: 丑禽牛，未禽羊, the branch chou belongs to the ox and wei to the sheep.[2]

In the Hong Kong Education Bureau's school version of the race, the Goat, the Monkey and the Rooster reach the river late and build a raft together. The Emperor is so pleased that they helped one another that he makes the Goat eighth, the Monkey ninth and the Rooster tenth.[3]

Museums use both English names. The Victoria and Albert Museum lists the eighth animal as the sheep[4], while The Metropolitan Museum of Art catalogues the Kangxi-period porcelain figure from its zodiac set as a goat.[5]

If someone asks 'Sheep or Goat?', both are correct translations of 羊; the years are identical.
