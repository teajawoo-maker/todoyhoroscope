Horse Chinese Zodiac: Horse Years 1930–2026, Exact Start Dates, Fire Element & Matches

The Horse, seventh of the twelve, takes the branch 午 (wǔ), which also names noon: the hour of the Horse runs from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m., and 午 sits at the centre of it. Its fixed element is Fire with yang polarity, which is why a Horse year with a Fire stem, like 2026, is a 'double Fire' year.

Horse compatibility in the classical system

The Horse completes the Fire trine (寅午戌) with the Tiger and the Dog. Its Liu He partner is the Goat (午未). The Rat sits exactly opposite and is its six-clash (子午), the north-south axis of the branch compass.

Horse in folklore and culture

The Horse loses sixth place at the very last moment. The British Library's version calls it a very good swimmer, but just as it reaches the finish line the Snake slithers out of its mane and takes sixth place.[2] In the story taught in Hong Kong schools the Horse is already on the bank, shaking off the water, when the Snake pops up; the startled Horse leaps backwards and is placed seventh.[3]

丙午, the Fire Horse, is read hinoe-uma in Japanese, and a folk belief held that a girl born in that year was a bad omen. Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare records 1,823,697 births in 1965, 1,360,974 in the Fire Horse year 1966 and 1,935,647 in 1967, a one-year fall of about 25%.[4] A 1975 paper in Annals of Human Biology puts the gap down partly to contraception and partly to induced abortion, which rose to 43.1 per 1,000 births against about 30.6 expected from the trend.[5] The previous Fire Horse year left a much smaller mark in the same table: 1,452,770 births in 1905 and 1,394,295 in 1906, roughly 4% fewer.[4]

The Victoria and Albert Museum traces the custom of giving each year an animal to at least the Han dynasty, and notes that figures with human bodies and animal heads became a popular way to show the twelve in the Tang dynasty.[6] The Metropolitan Museum of Art holds a complete 8th-century Tang set of the twelve animals, horse included, in earthenware with white slip.[7]

2026, 丙午, is the first Fire Horse year since 1966; the next one falls in 2086.
